## Four lenses, different explanatory roles

| Lens | Case connection | Analytical judgement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Appropriation | The source mentions learning from Futu and building an integrated platform | Benchmarking alone does not establish a new technological use or a changed user practice |
| Resource recombination | Research content, existing systems, a new department and customer-resource conflicts | A promising organisational mechanism, but the actual resources, connections and coordination process remain to be documented |
| Long Tail | Research previously directed at corporate clients is extended to a broader audience | A hypothesis about demand distribution and service economics, rather than a synonym for customer growth |
| Differentiation, examined through the resource-based view | The source presents differentiation as a transformation objective | Customer value, relative distinctiveness, imitation and substitution require separate evidence |

These lenses are not four obligatory stages. Appropriation concerns situated uses; recombination concerns service arrangements; the Long Tail concerns dispersed demand; differentiation concerns relative competitive outcomes. A service can exhibit one without satisfying the others. [Full conceptual bridge and primary references](docs/theory-case-bridge.md)

## A traceable reasoning example

**Evidence E07:** page 29 describes extending research content from corporate clients to a wider audience.

**Question:** does this support viable niche demand, or merely distribute the same popular material more widely?

**Proposition P2:** if reusable content becomes easier to discover, recurring use across niche topics may increase. Its commercial significance still depends on incremental review and support costs.

**Disconfirming observations:** demand remains concentrated in a few popular topics, or additional costs absorb the service contribution.

**Decision implication:** evaluate topic-level usage, repeated use and cost together, rather than treating total membership growth as proof of a Long Tail effect.

## What the work demonstrates

- Framing a bounded “how” question and an organisational unit of analysis.
- Operationalising concepts with case-specific evidence requirements.
- Integrating lenses without conflating levels, constructs or chronology.
- Considering rival explanations and observations that would weaken an interpretation.
- Deriving service-pilot decisions and evaluation criteria from the proposed mechanisms.
